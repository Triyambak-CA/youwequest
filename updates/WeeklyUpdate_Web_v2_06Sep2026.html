Direct Tax · Notification 114/2026, 14 August 2026 · Material
A one-time window to declare undisclosed foreign assets, open until 31 December
CBDT notified the rules and Forms 1 to 4 for the Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, 2026 (G.S.R. 732(E); the Scheme itself sits in Chapter IV, ss.130-144 of the Finance Act, 2026). The window opened 16 August 2026 and the declaration in Form 1 is due by 31 December 2026. Two categories: foreign assets never taxed, aggregate value up to Rs 1 crore, at 30% tax plus an additional amount equal to the tax, so 60% effective; and assets already taxed or from a non-resident source but simply unreported, up to Rs 5 crore, at a flat Rs 1 lakh. Value is taken at fair market value on 31 March 2026, and the test runs on asset value, not income. Read the immunity narrowly: it covers the Black Money (UFIA) Act, 2015 and nothing else - not FEMA, not PMLA, and not general prosecution under the Income-tax Act. Schedule FA reporting continues to apply from FY 2026-27 regardless.

ITEM 01 · FOREIGN ASSETS DISCLOSURE SCHEME · Material
01.1
Reference
CBDT Notification No. 114/2026, G.S.R. 732(E), F.No. 370142/18/2026-TPL, dated 14 August 2026, notifying the rules and forms. The Scheme itself is Chapter IV, ss.130-144 of the Finance Act, 2026 - s.131 eligibility, s.133 charge, s.135 procedure, s.140 exclusions. The rules were made under the s.143 rule-making power.
01.2
Window
Opened 16 August 2026. The declaration in Form 1 is due by 31 December 2026. Payment runs on a separate clock into 2027, with 1% per month on delay.
01.3
Forms
Form 1 - the declaration
Form 2 - the authority's order determining the amount payable
Form 3 - intimation of payment, with proof
Form 4 - the certificate of validity and immunity
01.4
Eligibility and cost
Category 1 - foreign assets never subjected to tax, aggregate value up to Rs 1 crore: tax at 30% plus an additional amount equal to the tax, an effective 60%
Category 2 - assets already taxed, or from a non-resident source, but not reported, aggregate value up to Rs 5 crore: a flat Rs 1 lakh
Both tested on fair market value at 31 March 2026, and on asset value rather than income
01.5
Limits of the immunity
This is the part clients will get wrong. The immunity runs to the Black Money (UFIA) Act, 2015 only. It does not extend to FEMA, to PMLA, or to general prosecution under the Income-tax Act, and Schedule FA reporting continues to apply from FY 2026-27 in the ordinary way. Describe the 100% addition as an additional amount equal to the tax, not as a penalty - the distinction matters if the charge is ever litigated.
01.6
Confirm on the notification
How a foreign bank account is valued. One reading takes the sum of all deposits since the account was opened, another the higher of cost or market value. The difference is decisive: on the deposits reading, a modest account can breach the Rs 1 crore ceiling.
Whether a search, survey or existing notice bars a declaration. The reported exclusions under s.140 are only PMLA proceeds of crime and years already assessed under the 2015 Act. Every prior Indian amnesty carried a detection bar, so treat its apparent absence with caution.
01.7
Action
Screen the client base now - overseas bank and brokerage accounts, ESOPs and RSUs in foreign parents, foreign pension entitlements and immovable property abroad - against Schedule FA history, then settle the two open points above. Read this alongside ITEM 02: the disclosure window and the Department's detection capability are moving together, and the window shuts 31 December 2026.
